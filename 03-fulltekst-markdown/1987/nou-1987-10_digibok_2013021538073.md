In the Cabinet meeting of 25 March 1985 the Government assented to this programme, which is described in the proposed budget for 1986, see Storting Proposition No. 1 for 1985-86, Ministry of Local Government and Labour, pages 30-31. An important part of this programme is to assess the suitability of the va rious methods, approaches and instruments that the authorities can employ. As part of the work of making the authorities' supervision more effective and improve safety, the working environment and safety admini strations of the Norwegian Petroleum Directo rate and the Directorate for Fire and Explosion Prevention have in recent years made require ments for so-called internal control in the com panies in order to enforce standards of working environment and safety. A certain amount of experience of internal control has no w been ac quired. Particularly within the petroleum indu stry both offshore and onshore, internal control is seen as a very major factor in the authorities' supervision. The Ministry thought it would be most inter esting and useful to undertake a thorough eva luation of this experience. Such an analysis will provide a better picture both of what criteria must be fulfilled for supervision systems based on internal control to work as designed, and the possibly weaker aspects of internal control. The Ministry thought that the time was now also ripe to look more closely at the question of whether internal control should be applied in areas where this kind of control method has not hitherto been employed. Source material for a thorough and balanced analysis on this point includes experience of the Norwegian Petro leum Directorate's and the Directorate for Fire and Explosion Prevention's activities. To quotefrom the mandate: The Committee is to treat internal control in the context of general official instruments in the administrative area concerned. Internal control is thus to be evaluated as an instrument for fulfilling the objects and missions of the ad ministration in relation to responsibilities and tasks and the considerations to be attended to, as well as to disposition of control resources in public bodies and in the enterprises (compani es, industry). It will also be natural to assess cost-effectiveness of internal control as an in strument with regard to adjustment and imple mentation of safety and working environment measures. The Committee is thus: - to describe the models of the Norwegian Pet roleum Directorate and the Directorate for Fire and Explosion Prevention for internal control, including conditions, assumptions, systems, functioning and characteristics. - to chart and evalutate the experiences to date (positive/negative effects) seen from the point of view of the authorities, the compani es and the workforce, describe and evaluate the developement of the models and their mode of operation (intended and unintended effects), and hereunder to assess administra tive and financial consequences. - in particular to evaluate the potential (advan tages) of internal control within the areas in which it is employed today, and hereunder to clarify what important criteria must be fulfil led, also to clarify and assess any limitations, critical or weak aspects. - to evaluate internal control as a general in strument for safety and working environ ment work, hereunder how the effectiveness of this instrument is dependent on various conditions, inter alia with regard to the for mulation of the regulations and supervision arrangements, plus financial and administra tive consequences. - to evaluate the general potential for use of in ternal control in other areas and propose a strategy for further development and imple mentation and point to likely areas for trials. NOU 1987:10 A Internal control in an integrated strategy for working environment and safety CHAPTER 2 Present working environment and safety management 2.1 OFFICIAL WORKING ENVIRONMENT AND SAFETY REQUIREMENTS 2.1.1 General The official supervisory bodies and their re gulations were developed to help ensure that safety and working environment is safeguarded in the best possible fashion. This means, inter alia, that the workforce is protected against ac cidents that present a hazard to life and health, and further that there is a satisfactory working environment, that is, that workers are not ex posed to harmful physical and mental stress and hazards. In the petroleum industry and in the fire and explosion prevention legislation the worker's physical working environment is covered by the term «safety», while in the Wor king Environment Act safety is included in the broader consept of «working environment». Since the Committee's mandate includes both areas, the expression «safety and working en vironment» is used. A distinction is often made between various types of input factors in an activity with regard to safety and working environment. These de monstrate different aspects of the activity that may be subjected to regulation with the object of attaining the goals laid down by the authoriti es. A number of these factors are presented be low, directly connected with the safety and working environment aspects of the activity. In addition to such direct input factors, attention may be paid to circumstances not directly rela ted to safety and working environment, but which are thought to have some significance for them. This applies particularly to circumstan ces linked to the manner in which the activity is organised, management systems and forms of worker participation. In order to further considerations of safety and working environment requirements are made concerning technical conditions, for ex ample buildings, machinery and equipement. To combat damage following accidents, struc tures are required to tolerate such stresses that accidents can be avoided. Requirements may also be made that plant and installations must be so designed that should accidents happen the consequences for the workers are minimis ed. For example, explosives manufacture is not to be located in the vicinity of canteens, schools, hospitals etc, but in areas where few if any pe ople are likely to be found (short exposure ti me). Structures and installations may also be required to withstand the consequences of an accident, for example by means of fireproof walls. Demands of a technical nature are also made of normal operations with the aim of keeping human stress in consequence of the operations under a certain level. This may apply to things lilke noise, temperature and atmospheric pollu tion in the form of dust and gas. In addition the re may be mentioned ergonomic factors such as muscle and posture strain. A separate group of requirements relates to various substances employed in the work pro cess. Certain substances are on account of their hazardous nature generally prohibited, for ex ample asbestos. Others may be subjected to special restrictions and requirements relating to use, treatment, storage and registration.
