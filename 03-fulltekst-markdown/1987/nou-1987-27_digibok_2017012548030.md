Part II CHAPTER 1 Introduction 1.1 INTRODUCTION This report comprises Part II of the report from the Committee on the Role of the Police concerning the role and duties of the police in Norwegian society. Part I of this report was submitted in November 1981. In Part I, the committee presented a relatively detailed de scription of the Norwegian police force today, the basis for its duties, the resources it has at its disposal and how it is organized in order to carry out its various duties. A summary of Part I has been translated to English (NOU 1981:35). The Central issue presented in Part I was the discussion concerning the basic principles go verning the activities of the police in Norway. In answer to the question of what kind of poli ce does Norway want, the committee listed ten principles: 1. The police shall reflect the ideals of society. 2. The police shall have a civilian profile. 3. We must have an integrated police. 4. The police shall be decentralized. 5. The police officer shall be a «generalist». 6. The police shall internet with the public. 7. The police shall be an integrated part of the local community. 8. Recruitment to the police shall represent a broad spectrum of society. 9. The police themselves shall organize their different tasks according to priority, placing emphasis on preventive activities. 10. The police shall be subject to effective control by society. Based on these principles, the committee discussed the funetion of the police in the fu ture. There is no reason to repeat that discussi on in this report; see Part I for speciflc details of the tasks which were reviewed by the com mittee. However, the committee determined that the most important funetion of the police is to maintain peace, order and security, which implies the obligation to ensure all citizens the right to be left in peace, and to be able to move about freely, unthreatened by infringe ments of their privaey. Part II of the committee’s report discusses the various pilot projects involving new met hods for improving police service which have been implemented during recent years. The summary projects which have been conducted have be en based partly on the initiative of the Committee on the Role of the Police and part ly on that of the Ministry of Justice. The committee has considered such projects to be a vital part of the process which is necessary if reforms within the police force are to come about. The report reviews projects involving the establishment of local police advisory bo ards to increase the contact between local communities and the police. The committee discusses how a decentrali zed police force should be organized. Included in this discussion is an assessment of the poli ce advisory boards, which were organized to include representatives from both the local authorities and the police. The committee pro poses that local police councils be established on a permanent basis. Police officers’ work roles and the working environment in the police and rural police Services are discussed. A survey of the wor king environment for the police was initiated by the committee. The results of this survey, which was conducted by researchers from the Institute of Work Psychology, are available in Norwegian from the Ministry of Justice or the Institute of Work Psychology. The committee discusses special problems and duties which the police have to deal with today, and which will still exist in the future as well. These involve problems associated with the police’s intervention in labour dispu tes and various types of protest actions and demonstrations. The committee reviews the economic and administrative ramifications of the proposals put forward in Part I and Part II of this re port. The committee presents its views concerning the proposals put forward by the Manpower Committee, a sub-committee appo inted to evaluate the manpower needs in the police and rural police Services. The commit tee majority supports the Manpower Commitassessments and conclusions. 1.2 SUMMARY 1.2.1 Projects utilizing new methods for poli ce Services During the Autumn of 1978, the Ministry of Justice implemented a pilot project entitled

In terms of the functions involved in the investigative Services, the committee reviews the means investigators have at their disposal for dealing with the individual cases. The meeting between investigator and offender is not characterized by the acute conflict so often encountered by the uniformed police service. Thus, the investigator’s work situation provides a better opportunity for le arning more about various aspects of the offender’s life in general. Thus, the investiga tor also has a greater chance of influencing the offender’s attitudes, which in turn may play a Central role in preventing later criminal acti ons. It is possible that this aspect of the criminal prosecution process actually provides much of the preventive effect which many ascribe to the punishment itself. In the committe e’s opinion, this distinction is of no importance in determining the extent to which the police should intervene. In the case of legal labour disputes, the parties are availing themselves of the right given them by legislators to utilize some form of industrial action in their efforts to win acceptance for their demands. In the case of illegal actions, there is a violation of the obligations which the parties have under taken in relation to one another, but the committee asserts that it is not unlawful to resort to industrial action in these situations either. The crucial point is whether, during the dis pute, the parties perform actions which are unlawful.
