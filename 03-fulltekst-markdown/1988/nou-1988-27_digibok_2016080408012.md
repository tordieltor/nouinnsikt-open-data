Foreword The Governmental Commission on Norway’s international goods transports was appointed by Order in Council on 13 December 1985. The Commission’s recommendation was presented to the Ministry of Transport and Communications on 19 September 1988. The Commission’s primary task has been to research measures in the freight transport sector which could be instrumental in strengthe ning trade and industry that are exposed to competition, and ensure that, as far as transport is concerned, both the import and export of goods are effected as efficiently as possible. The report covers mainland Norway’s foreign trade in commoditi es, i.e. the transport of «traditional» goods to and from Norway. The transport of oil, gas, ships and oil platforms is not included. The public authorities and trade and industry are making special efforts in several areas to generate growth in traditional industries in Nor way, which have remained stagnant for several years, eclipsed by a sharply expanding petroleum sector. Efficient transport and good delivery service are becoming increasingly important as tools to that end. The report deals with both measures controlled by the state, and recommendations on which the transporters and the goods owners themselves can act. The bulk of the research work was carried out in 1986 and 1987. During this period, contact was maintained with the parties concer ned, such as through regular information regarding ad hoc measures and provisional conclusions. A number of the Committee’s special comments issued in the course of the research work were followed up to some extent before the recommendation was presented.

Transport and Competitive Ability THE TASKS OF THE COMMISSION The report focuses on the needs and pro blems of the owners of goods as purchasers of transport Services. Each transport business has been studied in relation to the Services it offers to the goods-producing industries. The competition between the various modes of transport in Norway has not been included in the scope of the report. The Committee’s most important tasks we re: 1. To map the flow of goods and transport in Norway’s foreign commodity trade. 2. To analyze the cost of transport, storage and distribution in foreign trade. 3. To re vie w administrative and legal pro blems in connection with transfrontier traffic that may impede an effective ex change of goods. 4. To review international trade and transport policy agreements that may reduce the effi ciency of international transport. 5. To review developments in transport and Communications technology and interna tional standardization work with a view to appropriate Norwegian modifications. 6. Evaluate current plans for investment in transport and Communications on the basis of the needs of the foreign commodity tra de. Most of the proposals in the recommendati on involve changes in the country’s economic conditions. This applies, among other things, to infrastructure, competitive policy, interna tional cooperation and research and develop ment. The report contains no proposals for dramatic changes in government policy. On the other hand, certain modifications are sug gested, which, seen as a whole, will be instru mental in strengthening Norwegian trade and industry’s ability to compete in the internatio nal m arket. The efficiency and quality of the transporta tion market are determined primarily by the parties actively involved. The recommendati on evaluates measures affecting both trans port companies and transport users. However, the recommendation is not an instruction manual for concrete corporate modifications, but is aimed at creating a better recognition in trade and industry of the role of transport and delivery service as a competitive factor. Norway’s international freight transport Ser vices have not been studied before. Besides suggesting measures to enhance efficiency and improve delivery service, emphasis has also been placed on producing statistics and other information regarding the flow of goods and transport, as well as logistics costs in fo reign trade. CHALLENGES AND PROBLEMS Efficient transport and first-class delivery service - reduced costs and increased reve nues Efficient transport and delivery service are becoming increasingly important as a compe titive factor in international trade. Norway’s export-oriented economy and strong depen dence on foreign markets for both buying and selling underscores the significance of effici ent international transport. The great distan ces separating Norway from our most important markets accentuate the need for effective Solutions. The trade and transport of commodities are inextricably bound together. This is the case within each individual country as well as in international relations. Despite this obvious connection, many producers, exporters and importers, and government officials, still have a tendency to consider and deal with trade transactions and freight transport as separate activities. The efficient administration of materials, or logistics, frees Capital and results in increased profitability. By systematically planning and coordinating the flow of material and goods from the raw material source to the end user, one can obtain an overall flow of goods with lower total costs and a higher quality of deli very service. Thus a slight increase in trans port costs may be remunerative if, through such factors as better delivery service, it re sults in increased revenues.

Transport and Competitive Ability Both trade and industry and the govern ment are working to achieve a change in the production of goods towards the production and export of products with a higher degree of processing. This requires a considerable effort since a very substantial portion of Nor wegian export goods go to markets with relatively little growth, and since the increase in international trade particularly involves products which have hitherto played a limited role in Norway’s foreign trade. The challenge lies in introducing new products to rapidly growing markets. This necessitates, among other things, efficient marketing and distribu tion systems, which may result in logistic costs that are slightly higher, but which will be more than offset by increased revenues. Continued economic growth and more inter national work collaboration (specialization, sub-contracting), will help to foster a more extensive exchange of goods across national frontiers. Moreover, the change to more pro cessed goods will call for higher-quality transportation and better delivery service. Transport costs Storage costs \//\ Other costs The transport costs are the most important component, amounting to almost 60 percent of logistics costs, while storage costs represent about one-third. Other costs, such as insuran ce, transport packaging and costs of Capital for goods during transport, are relatively less sig nificant. It is primarily in the sectors of transport and storage, and in coordinating these two areas, that efflciency measures will be able to make an impact. The logistics costs of various trades vary substantially, depending on the value of the goods, requirements as to transport mode and the distance to the markets. These variations are also reflected in the way the costs are di stributed among various components. For example, fresh farm-raised fish must be ex ported with no storage time. Here transport costs are the dominant factor. Packaging costs also account for a relatively large share of to tal logistics costs. In the woodworking busi ness, however, there is a better balance between transport and storage costs. Higher logistics costs than countries with which we compete? There are indications that Norwegian export trade and industry have higher logistics costs than comparable lines of business in other countries. The distances between us and our most important markets are a fundamental drawback in terms of costs. High-cost pro blems are also a result of the population pattern, the trade structure and a generally high level of costs. Lower standards of trans port, higher taxes and a poorer utilization of modes of transport than in important competi tor countries are also significant factors. Measures to reduce logistics costs will result in considerable gains. It is estimated that a 10-percent reduction of export businesses’ transport and Communi cations costs will lead to a rise in operating profits of an average 20 percent. However, operating profits in export industries fluctuate with the business cycles in international mar kets, so that the impact on profitability in any one given year does not necessarily provide a good picture. Nevertheless, a reduction of that type can be compared with the effect of a decrease in wage costs of about 2.5 percent. CONCLUSIONS AND RECOMMENDATIONS Adaptation of economic conditions: free tra de and free transport. A prerequisite of free trade is the free flow of goods across national frontiers. Transport policies must therefore be based on the same principles as international trade. Free trade and a heavily regulated transport market will, in the long term, lead to reduced efficiency and diminished competitiveness in trade and industry. The desire to protect certain indu stries and national markets must be seen in the light of such far-reaching effects. Access to the EC’s common transport market is the most important transport policy modi fication. The European Common Market (EC) is the most important market area in Norwegian fo reign trade. The EC’s inner market is to be established by 1993, including a common transport market with free competition in con ditions that are as similar as possible. The biggest changes are taking place in the field of road transport. The EC is pursuing two main lines of action to establish a common road transport market: In part, liberalization of access to the market, in part, harmonization of terms of competiti on. Easing access to the market and dismant ling the strict regulatory system goyerning international transport is the primary goal in order to create a common transport market. Harmonizing terms of competition, i.e. vehicle taxation, authorized weights and dimensions, and commercial drivers’ driving and rest peri ods, is important and necessary, but does not, as such, create a common transport market. The market is being liberalized primarily through annual increases in the number of third-country transport permits issued by the EC. Transport companies are to be granted permits on the basis of professional qualificati ons rather than an evaluation of the markefs transport capacity requirements. The free market shall be established even if the EC does not achieve the simultaneous harmoniza tion of road traffic taxes. This is probably because that the EC has realized that road traf fic taxes do not have as great a relative signifi cance as earlier assumed. The EC’s common transport market will le ad to greater competition, a more versatile range of transport Services, better utilization of transport modes, and lower transport costs. Norwegian businesses’ competitors in the EC will benefit from a competitive advantage if Norway must continue to base its internatio nal transport on bilateral quotas and other restraints which limit the choices and efficien cy in the transport market. Companies in the EC countries where technical, physical and tax barriers to free transport and exchange of goods have been abolished, will more easily be able to meet the stiffer demands for effici ent transport and satisfactory delivery service than companies situated outside the inner market. The establishment of the inner mar ket will also accentuate the disadvantages of our peripheral location in relation to the mar kets on the Continent. The most rational decision to make to attain transport policy goals would be to apply for membership in the EC. However, the Com mission is aware of the fact that no standpoint has been reached on that issue as yet, and has therefore considered other Solutions.
